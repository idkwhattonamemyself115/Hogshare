<section id="problem">
<h2>The Problem: Pay more, use less</h2>
<p>NMH students regularly encounter a recurring and inefficient cycle of consumption. Before major campus events like Dwight Night, athletic banquets, and formal dances, students often spend $70 or more on items like dresses and accessories that are worn only once. Similarly, students seeking weekend entertainment frequently make impulsive purchases of recreational goods such as sports equipment or gaming consoles only for the items to be left alone shortly after. &ldquo;I sometimes really don&rsquo;t want to spend that much on a new costume, but there&rsquo;s no way I&rsquo;m re-wearing my old ones, so I guess I have to,&rdquo; said one of the initial students interviewed. Not only is this one-time-use consumption making students pay more, but it also creates excessive waste on campus. The waste thus leads to negative environmental impacts, as many one-time-use plastics and fabrics cause severe environmental harms and take decades to degrade.</p>
<figure><img class="photo" src="images/dresses.jpg" alt="Students in dresses on church steps" loading="lazy"><figcaption>Dresses (example of how many times all of these dresses could be worn)</figcaption></figure>
<p>According to our user survey, more than 80% of responders said they wanted something for an event but didn&rsquo;t buy it because of its price, and 37% of all responders said they have a habit of borrowing items from other users. Therefore, the other side of this unhappy equilibrium is that students who require temporary access to items have no reliable or structured method for doing so beyond informal peer-to-peer requests. Currently, this need is met inadequately. There is no existing system that creates a mutually beneficial exchange between students with inactive assets and students seeking short-term access. This is a persistent gap. Students want to regain some of their initial investment, but have no platform to do so, and students who want affordable, temporary access to goods have no efficient way to find them.</p>
</section>

<section id="mission">
<h2>Our mission: A circular system built for NMH</h2>
<p>Hogshare&rsquo;s purpose is to bridge this gap of unmet needs for both sides. We are a solution that combines convenience, affordability, and sustainability within a single on-campus platform. As a social enterprise, Hogshare empowers students to lend and borrow clothing, dresses, themed costumes, accessories, sports gear, textbooks, and recreational equipment directly from one another. By shifting our campus culture from single-use ownership to shared access, Hogshare reduces waste, builds transparency &amp; trust, and strengthens our sustainability awareness as a student body.</p>
</section>

<section id="hogcash">
<h2>No, you will not pay a penny. Yes, we are GIVING OUT money &ndash;the Hogcash system</h2>
<p>Students are able to borrow items for FREE, and even make money out of transactions. At Hogshare, all transactions will be completed using Hogcash, a type of credit for borrowing and renting (1 Hogcash=$1 in rental value). Here are some ways you will interact with our system:</p>
<p><strong>Earn Hogcash:</strong> Our model encourages students to volunteer in a series of sustainability events and commit to environmentally friendly actions in return for script money. For example, students can donate clothing, attend sustainability events, or even leave a comment on Hogshare to earn Hogcash.</p>
<p><strong>Borrow what you need:</strong> Students can rent anything published on Hogshare with their Hogcash, which is a credit used internally for our platform.</p>
<p><strong>Make money out of Hogshare:</strong> Students can register and list their own items. Whenever their product is rented out to someone, the value of that transaction will be converted into available credits for the school store at specific cashout time frames. For example, if someone paid 5 Hogcash to you to borrow your hair curler, you get $5 credits in the school store. Therefore, students will be able to make real money by committing to volunteering and building a shared access community using Hogshare.</p>
</section>

<section id="progress">
<h2>Our progress so far:</h2>
<h3>Product deliverables: website prototype</h3>
<p><a href="https://drive.google.com/file/d/1n0TmOArrzh1XWz3ofIiA-UiC1OdJ0LqE/view">My Movie 27.mp4</a></p>
<p>Over the past two months, we designed and built a functioning website used for all Hogshare activities. It focuses on functionality, has many features that benefit the user experience, and is easy to navigate.</p>
<h3>Core features that we have:</h3>
<p><strong>User registration &amp; liability form:</strong> Users can sign up and log in to their account with their NMH email and a safe password. We acquired Google email verification access, so when users forget their password, Softr can send a one-time verification code to reset their password for safety considerations. Upon registration, we require all users to agree to our liability form, which includes return responsibility, financial responsibility for lost/damaged items, photo documentation requirement, and general etiquette for using Hogshare. The liability form is also present on our website page for users&rsquo; reference if needed.</p>
<p><strong>Item listings:</strong> When users sign in, they can freely navigate the list of items published by other users. Items are filtered based on condition, categories, and name. Users can list an item by listing its price, description, condition, and uploading a photo of the item in its current condition.</p>
<p><strong>Send request:</strong> When users want to borrow an item, they click the send request button on the listings page and fill out the specified request (dates, estimated prices, purposes, etc.) to the renter. The request will instantly show up on the renters&rsquo; side for them to approve/decline. When a request is approved, the users will be expected to contact each other through email and agree on a time and place on campus to meet to receive/return the item.</p>
<p><strong>Financial analytics</strong>: The main user interface shows the statistical analytics of historic payments, categorizing all transactions by category and type. It includes pie charts, bar graphs, and other visual representations that help users understand their transaction types and keep track of payment history. The main page also shows new requests for users to approve, requests the user made, and historical transactions. The purpose is to make renting requests more convenient and easy to navigate.</p>
</section>

<h2>Research:</h2>
<figure class="narrow"><img class="photo" src="images/focus-group.jpg" alt="Focus group" loading="lazy"><figcaption>Focus group</figcaption></figure>
